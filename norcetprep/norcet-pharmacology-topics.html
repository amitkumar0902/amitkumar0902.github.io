NORCET Pharmacology — High-Yield Topics

Pharmacology is a scoring differentiator in NORCET because the same facts repeat: antidotes, drug classes, side effects, contraindications, and nursing responsibilities (vital signs to monitor, labs, patient education). Below is a topic checklist aligned with common nursing competitive exams in India. Pair it with our 70+ free pharmacology MCQs.

1. Antidotes & poisoning

Master pairs: organophosphates → atropine + pralidoxime; paracetamol overdose → N-acetylcysteine; opioids → naloxone; benzodiazepines → flumazenil; heparin → protamine; warfarin → vitamin K / PCC context; iron → deferoxamine; digoxin toxicity → digoxin-specific Fab (concept level for exams). Know symptoms of toxicity (e.g., lithium, digoxin) and monitoring (CBC for clozapine).

2. Antimicrobials

Penicillins, cephalosporins generations, aminoglycosides (nephro/ototoxicity), macrolides, fluoroquinolones, metronidazole coverage, and anti-TB RIPE (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) with major side effects (hepatotoxicity, optic neuritis, etc.). Vancomycin “red man syndrome” and infusion rate.

3. Cardiovascular & antihypertensives

ACE inhibitors (cough, hyperkalemia), ARBs, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, diuretic classes, nitrates, antidysrhythmics at overview level. Anticoagulant/antiplatelet differences and reversal agents.

4. Endocrine & diabetes

Insulin types (rapid, short, NPH, long-acting), hypoglycemia management, oral antidiabetics class-wise, levothyroxine administration timing, corticosteroid adverse effects.

5. CNS & psychiatry

Antidepressants (SSRI mechanism, serotonin syndrome awareness), antipsychotics (EPS, metabolic effects), mood stabilizers (lithium levels), anti-epileptics (phenytoin kinetics, teratogenicity concepts).

6. Analgesics & anesthesia-related

Opioid vs NSAID vs multimodal analgesia; tramadol uniqueness; local anesthetic systemic toxicity awareness; pre-medications at basic level.

7. Emergency & ICU drugs

Adrenaline dosing context in arrest, atropine for bradycardia, amiodarone overview, vasopressors/inotropes at conceptual level, calcium in hyperkalemia with ECG changes.

8. Nursing process in pharmacology

Five rights, medication reconciliation, Z-track IM, sublingual nitroglycerin storage, crushing tablets contraindications, IV compatibility checks, and patient teaching for high-risk drugs.

9. OTC, herbals & interactions

Exams often touch patient self-medication: NSAID + anticoagulant risk, St. John’s wort with antidepressants (enzyme induction), PPIs affecting absorption, and counseling on reading labels. Know when to escalate to the physician.

10. Dose calculation & safety checks

Review mg/kg basics, rounding for pediatric liquids, and “double-check” high-alert meds (insulin, heparin, chemo). NORCET may embed simple arithmetic or “which order is unsafe?” scenarios — practice translating prescription language to nursing action.

11. NORCET pharmacology MCQ free — how to rank your own prep

Many aspirants search for free NORCET pharmacology MCQ or NORCET pharmacology questions online because paid apps lock full tests behind subscriptions. This hub keeps pharmacology practice in the browser: no account, no payment, with short explanations after each item. That helps both speed (recognition of repeat patterns) and accuracy (knowing why an option is wrong).

Weekly rhythm: Monday–Wednesday, 20–30 fresh MCQs from the pharmacology bank. Thursday, revise mistakes only. Friday, mixed recall from this checklist (close the tab and write antidote pairs from memory). Weekend, one timed block from Stage 1 mock to feel time pressure. If a question references a drug you have never seen, add it to a one-page “drug diary” with class, key side effect, and nursing action.

High-return NORCET themes: (1) Antidotes and poisoning — fast points if memorized as pairs. (2) Anti-TB and hepatitis monitoring — rifampicin colour changes, INH neuropathy, ethambutol vision. (3) Anticoagulants — warfarin vs DOAC reversal concepts, heparin vs LMWH, bleeding protocols at outline level. (4) Insulin & hypoglycemia — onset/peak basics, hypoglycemia treatment steps.
