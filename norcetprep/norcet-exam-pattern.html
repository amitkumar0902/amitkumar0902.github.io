NORCET 10 Exam Pattern — Stage 1 vs Stage 2

AIIMS NORCET is conducted in two stages. Exact section names, subject-wise splits, and interface behaviour (e.g., sectional timing, locking) must be verified from the official NORCET 10 information bulletin and your admit card instructions. The pattern below reflects commonly reported structure for recent cycles and what our free mock tests simulate.

Stage 1 (screening / prelims style)

Typically 100 multiple-choice questions in about 90 minutes. Questions often mix nursing clinical content with general knowledge, reasoning, and sometimes English / aptitude components depending on the official scheme. Negative marking of one-third (−0.33 per wrong answer per mark scheme) is standard in many AIIMS MCQ papers — confirm for your cycle. Time per question averages about 54 seconds, so flagging, skipping, and batching GK/reasoning can matter as much as clinical knowledge.

Preparation focus: speed + accuracy on high-frequency nursing topics plus daily GK capsules. Use GK MCQs and Reasoning alongside clinical banks.

Stage 2 (mains / clinical emphasis)

Stage 2 is longer and often more clinical and integrated: commonly cited figures include 160 questions in 180 minutes (~67.5 seconds per question). Items may include longer stems, priority questions, drug interactions, diagnostic reasoning, and community health scenarios. Again, verify the official bulletin for NORCET 10.

Preparation focus: deep work in Med-Surg, Pharmacology, Anatomy & Physiology, and Community Health. Solve PYQ-style items under timer.

Qualifying marks (general guidance)

Information bulletins often specify category-wise minimum qualifying criteria (e.g., separate thresholds for UR/EWS, OBC, SC/ST as percentages of maximum marks). Treat any third-party summary as tentative until you read the PDF from AIIMS. Our hub homepage displays illustrative cutoffs for motivation only — not a substitute for the official document.

Section lock practice

Some CBT exams prevent backward movement between sections after submission. Our mock UI lets you submit a section and then locks prior sections to rehearse that constraint. If the real exam allows free navigation, you can still use section submit as a self-imposed discipline drill.
