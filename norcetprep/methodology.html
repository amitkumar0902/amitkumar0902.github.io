NurseDrill▚ Methodology

How our questions are made, checked, and corrected

Nursing-exam prep has a trust problem: invented faculty, invented ranks, invented authority. The only useful answer is to show the process. This is ours, written plainly, including the parts that are limitations.

Who writes this

NurseDrill is built by Amit Kumar, an engineer, working solo. There is no institute behind it and no panel of professors. What there is: a written process, source citations on every question, and a public record of every error we have fixed. If you'd rather judge the work than the credentials, that is the intended way to read this page.

Questions, corrections, and complaints: contact page.

The pipeline, stage by stage

Stage 1
Blueprint, not volume

New questions are commissioned against a blueprint derived from what the exam has actually asked — subject weights and topic frequencies taken from past NORCET Mains papers, not from a wish list. A topic that has appeared in eight of nine papers gets more questions than one that appeared once. We would rather ship 40 questions on the right topics than 400 on the wrong ones.

Stage 2
Automated consistency checks

Every question passes machine validators before a human ever sees it. They reject: a missing or out-of-range answer key, options that aren't exactly four, a missing explanation, a missing source citation, two options that say the same thing, a catch-all "all of the above" that makes the key ambiguous, and — the useful one — an explanation that contradicts its own answer key. These run on every content change, and a failure blocks the change.

Run them yourself: the validators are in the public repository (scripts/validate-questions.mjs).

Stage 3
Independent cross-verification

Each question, its answer key, and its explanation are then put to an independent automated reviewer — deliberately a different system from the one used to draft — with one instruction: refute this. Argue the key is wrong, argue a second option is equally defensible, argue the guideline has moved. Anything it refutes, and anything it is unsure about, goes into a review queue rather than into the product. The tool is itself tested against a set of deliberately broken questions it has to catch.

Stage 4
Human review

Every flagged question is reviewed by hand — 100% of them, no sampling — plus a random 20% of everything that passed cleanly, because a gate you never audit is a gate you don't know the strength of. Reviewed items record what was checked and when. Where a fact is genuinely contested between guidelines (they exist — resuscitation drug timing is the classic), we say so in the explanation instead of pretending there is one answer.

Stage 5
A source citation on every question

Every question names the reference that governs the fact it tests — a standard textbook chapter or a national guideline: Park for community health, Brunner & Suddarth for medical-surgical, KDT for pharmacology, Dutta for obstetrics, Ghai for paediatrics, the MoHFW rules and programme documents where the fact is Indian policy. This is enforced by a validator, so it cannot quietly lapse.

The limitation, stated plainly: citations are at chapter or guideline level, not page level. We would rather give you a reference you can verify than a page number we invented.

Stage 6
Your reports, and the public fix-log

Every question in the product has a report control. Reports are triaged weekly; fixes ship with the weekly publish, and each one is written into the public fix-log with a date, what was wrong, and what changed. Nothing is quietly edited. If we got something wrong and you told us, the record of that is public.

Previous-year papers

Every previous-year set on NurseDrill is memory-based recall: questions reconstructed from what candidates remember, reworded by us, with explanations we wrote. We do not reproduce official question papers — they are copyrighted, and we won't build a product on someone else's paper. Where a recall set is incomplete or a remembered option looks wrong, the explanation says so.

Practical consequence: a recall set is excellent for pattern and pressure, and imperfect as a source of truth. Treat the explanation and citation as the authority, not the recalled option.

What we don't claim

  • No "expert-verified" badge. No credentialed reviewer works on this today; if that changes, this page will name them.
  • No selection or rank promises. Nobody can sell you a rank.
  • No affiliation with AIIMS, and no use of their marks. Exam names are descriptive.
  • No claim of being error-free. A live question bank has errors; the honest measures are how fast they are found and whether the fixes are visible. That's what the fix-log is for.

The weekly rhythm

During exam season a new full mock ships each week. It goes through every stage above. If a mock fails its gates, it ships late rather than unreviewed — and we say so when it happens. Off-season the cadence is slower, and we'd rather print that than pretend otherwise.

Support runs on printed limits too: replies within 48 hours, payment and access problems handled the same day, fixes batched weekly. Details on the contact page.
