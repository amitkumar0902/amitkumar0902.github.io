TEF — Tracheoesophageal Fistula

Most Common Type

  • Type C (85%) — Proximal esophageal atresia + Distal TEF

Classic "3 C's" During Feeding

Coughing · Choking · Cyanosis — triggered by feeding
  • Excessive drooling / foamy secretions
  • Inability to pass NG tube into stomach
  • Diagnosis: Failure to pass catheter, X-ray with coiled catheter in esophagus

Pre-op Nursing

  • Keep NPO
  • Continuous low suction of blind pouch
  • Elevate HOB 30–45°
  • IV fluids, respiratory support, prevent aspiration
VACTERL association: Vertebral · Anorectal · Cardiac · TEF · Esophageal · Renal · Limb anomalies

G6PD Deficiency

  • Inheritance: X-linked recessive (males predominantly affected)
  • Pathophysiology: Oxidative stress → hemolysis

Triggers (AVOID)

  • Fava beans (classic)
  • Sulfonamides (Bactrim)
  • Antimalarials (primaquine, chloroquine)
  • Aspirin, nitrofurantoin
  • Naphthalene (mothballs)
  • Infections

Lab Findings

  • Heinz bodies & bite cells on blood smear
  • Dark urine, jaundice, pallor during crisis
  • Low haptoglobin, ↑ LDH, ↑ indirect bilirubin

Management

  • Avoid triggers (patient education is priority)
  • Supportive care · transfusion if severe
  • Folic acid supplementation
1.5 hours

Sickle Cell Disease

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive — HbS
  • RBCs sickle under stress → vaso-occlusion → ischemia

Sickling Triggers (5 H's + Stress)

5 H's: Hypoxia · Hypothermia (cold) · Hypovolemia (dehydration) · High altitude · Hyper-exertion · Infection · Stress

Crises

CrisisDescription
Vaso-occlusive (pain)Most common; severe pain in bones, abdomen, chest
Splenic sequestrationRapid splenic pooling → shock; seen in young children
Aplastic crisisTriggered by Parvovirus B19 → bone marrow shutdown
Hemolytic crisisRapid RBC destruction → jaundice
Acute Chest SyndromeFever + chest pain + new infiltrate on X-ray → medical emergency

Nursing Management

  • Hydration — IV fluids
  • Oxygen — maintain SpO₂ >95%
  • Pain management — often requires IV morphine/opioids
  • Incentive spirometry (prevent ACS)
  • Avoid cold; warm compresses (NEVER cold packs)
  • Hydroxyurea — increases HbF, reduces sickling
  • Penicillin prophylaxis for children <5y (prevent pneumococcal sepsis)

Hemophilia

FeatureHemophilia AHemophilia B (Christmas disease)
Deficient factorFactor VIII (most common, 80%)Factor IX
InheritanceX-linked recessiveX-linked recessive
Classic treatmentFactor VIII concentrate · DDAVP for mild casesFactor IX concentrate

Clinical Signs

  • Hemarthrosis — joint bleeding (knees, elbows, ankles) → chronic arthropathy
  • Easy bruising, prolonged bleeding after minor trauma
  • Intramuscular hematomas

Lab Findings

  • Prolonged PTT
  • Normal PT, platelets, bleeding time
  • Specific factor assay confirms type

Nursing Care (High-Yield)

  • AVOID aspirin & NSAIDs (use paracetamol for pain)
  • NO IM injections — use subcutaneous or IV route
  • Apply firm pressure to puncture sites for 5–10 minutes
  • Soft toothbrush to prevent gum bleeds
  • Electric razor rather than blade
  • Avoid contact sports (swimming is ideal)
  • Medic-alert bracelet
