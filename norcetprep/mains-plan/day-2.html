ET Tube Sizing

Uncuffed tube: (Age in years / 4) + 4
Cuffed tube: (Age / 4) + 3.5
Depth of insertion (oral): ETT internal diameter × 3

Age/Weight-Based ETT Sizes

PatientETT Size (mm)
Preterm neonate2.0 – 2.5
Term neonate3.0 – 3.5
6-month infant3.5 – 4.0
1 year4.0 – 4.5
Adult female7.0 – 7.5
Adult male8.0 – 8.5
Clinical Pearls
  • Always keep 0.5 mm above and below the calculated size ready.
  • Most common mal-position: right mainstem bronchus (right main bronchus is more vertical).
  • Confirm placement by capnography (gold standard) + bilateral breath sounds + chest rise + X-ray.
  • NORCET PYQ: Wheezing after ET tube → think bronchospasm or laryngospasm.
3 hours

Drug Calculation

Desired dose formula: (Desired / Have) × Vehicle = Amount to give
IV Flow rate (gtt/min): (Volume × Drop factor) / Time in minutes
mL/hr: Total volume / Total time in hours
Microdrip: gtt/min = mL/hr (drop factor 60)

Drop Factors

  • Macrodrip sets: 10, 12, 15, or 20 gtt/mL (check tubing label)
  • Microdrip (pediatric): 60 gtt/mL — microdrip gtt/min = mL/hr

High-Alert Calculation Examples

  • Heparin drip — weight-based, units/hr (titrated by aPTT)
  • Insulin drip — units/hr, sliding scale interpretation
  • Pediatric dosing — mg/kg/dose — always check safe dose range
  • Vasopressors (dopamine, noradrenaline) — mcg/kg/min
  • Potassium IV: NEVER bolus. Dilute. Max rate 10 mEq/hr peripheral, 20 mEq/hr central.

Worked NORCET-Style Examples

Example 1: Order — Ampicillin 750 mg IV. Available — 1 g/5 mL. Give → (750 / 1000) × 5 = 3.75 mL.
Example 2: Infuse 1000 mL D5W over 8 hours using 15 gtt/mL set. Rate → (1000 × 15) / (8 × 60) = 31 gtt/min (NORCET 9 PYQ answer).
Example 3: 100 mL over 30 min via 60 gtt/mL microdrip. Rate → (100 × 60) / 30 = 200 gtt/min = 200 mL/hr.
Example 4: Child 12 kg needs paracetamol 15 mg/kg. Dose → 12 × 15 = 180 mg. If syrup is 120 mg/5 mL → (180/120) × 5 = 7.5 mL.
30-Problem Practice Set: 10 IV rate · 10 weight-based peds · 5 drip titration · 5 unit conversion. Aim for <1 min per problem, 100% accuracy.

Choice of IV Fluids — RL, NS, DNS, D5W

FluidCompositionBest UseCaution
Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) Na⁺ 154, Cl⁻ 154 — isotonic Vomiting/gastric losses, metabolic alkalosis, TBI, blood transfusion carrier Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis with large volumes
Ringer's Lactate (RL) Na, Cl, K, Ca, lactate — isotonic Burns (Parkland), sepsis, diarrhea, biliary/pancreatic loss, metabolic acidosis correction Avoid in severe liver failure & TBI (lactate converts to HCO₃)
DNS (Dextrose Normal Saline) 0.9% NS + 5% dextrose Maintenance fluid, calorie provision Avoid in hyperglycemia
D5W 5% dextrose in water Drug dilution, free water replacement NOT for resuscitation (becomes hypotonic after dextrose is metabolized)
3% Hypertonic Saline Hypertonic Severe symptomatic hyponatremia (Na <120) Risk of central pontine myelinolysis if corrected >12 mEq/L per 24h
Remember: Vomiting → NS · Burns / diarrhea → RL · Hyponatremia 120 → 3% saline · Hyponatremia 132 → 0.9% NS · Hypoglycemia with TPN off → D10W
NORCET Exam Tip: When stem mentions "metabolic alkalosis + vomiting" → answer is almost always Normal Saline. When stem is "burn" or "massive diarrhea" → Ringer's Lactate.
