ABG Interpretation

Normal Values (MEMORIZE)

  • pH: 7.35 – 7.45
  • PaCO₂: 35 – 45 mmHg
  • HCO₃⁻: 22 – 26 mEq/L
  • PaO₂: 80 – 100 mmHg
  • SpO₂: 95 – 100%
ROME Mnemonic:
Respiratory = Opposite (pH ↑ → CO₂ ↓ and vice-versa)
Metabolic = Equal (pH ↑ → HCO₃ ↑ and vice-versa)

4-Step Method

  1. Check pH — Acidosis (<7.35) or Alkalosis (>7.45)?
  2. Check PaCO₂ — Respiratory cause?
  3. Check HCO₃ — Metabolic cause?
  4. Determine compensation — full / partial / none

High-Yield Scenarios

Clinical PictureDiagnosis
COPD patient, chronic CO₂ retainerRespiratory acidosis (compensated)
Persistent vomiting / NG suction lossMetabolic alkalosis
DKA, Kussmaul breathingMetabolic acidosis
Rib fracture, hypoventilationRespiratory acidosis
Panic attack, hyperventilationRespiratory alkalosis
Severe diarrhea (HCO₃ loss)Metabolic acidosis
Practice: Solve 20 ABG problems using the Tic-Tac-Toe method. Include NORCET 7 classic: pH 7.25, HCO₃ 30, PCO₂ 50 → Partially compensated respiratory acidosis.

ECG Interpretation

Normal Intervals

  • P wave — atrial depolarization (≤0.12s)
  • PR interval — 0.12 – 0.20s (3–5 small boxes)
  • QRS complex — 0.06 – 0.10s (ventricular depolarization)
  • T wave — ventricular repolarization
  • QT interval — 0.36 – 0.44s

Heart Blocks — 1st / 2nd / 3rd Degree

BlockClassic FeatureTreatment
1st degreePR > 0.20s (prolonged, consistent)Observation
2nd degree Type I (Wenckebach / Mobitz I)PR progressively lengthens → QRS droppedUsually benign; atropine if symptomatic
2nd degree Type II (Mobitz II)PR constant → sudden QRS dropPacemaker (often permanent)
3rd degree (Complete AV block)No P-QRS relationship; independent atrial & ventricular rhythmsTransvenous pacing → permanent pacemaker

Life-Threatening Rhythms

VT (Ventricular Tachycardia): Wide QRS, regular, HR >150.
  • Stable + pulse: Amiodarone IV
  • Unstable + pulse: Synchronized cardioversion
  • Pulseless VT: Defibrillation (unsynchronized) + CPR + Epi 1 mg
VF (Ventricular Fibrillation): Chaotic, no identifiable waves, no pulse.
Treatment: Immediate defibrillation + CPR + Epinephrine 1 mg after 3rd shock + Amiodarone 300 mg.

WPW Syndrome (Wolff-Parkinson-White)

  • Short PR (<0.12s)
  • Delta wave — slurred QRS upstroke
  • Wide QRS
  • AVOID: AV nodal blockers — Adenosine, Digoxin, Verapamil (can ↑ accessory pathway conduction → VF)

Electrolyte ECG Changes

ElectrolyteECG Finding
HyperkalemiaTall peaked T waves → wide QRS → sine wave → asystole
HypokalemiaFlat/inverted T wave, prominent U wave, ST depression
HypercalcemiaShort QT interval
HypocalcemiaProlonged QT interval
NORCET PYQ: Hyperkalemia + tall T waves → Calcium gluconate (stabilizes cardiac membrane).

Burns

Rule of Nines (Adult)

  • Head & Neck — 9%
  • Each Arm — 9% (total upper limbs 18%)
  • Anterior Trunk — 18%
  • Posterior Trunk — 18%
  • Each Leg — 18% (total lower limbs 36%)
  • Perineum — 1%
Parkland Formula:   4 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA burned = Total RL for 24 hrs
→ 50% in first 8 hours from time of burn
→ 50% in next 16 hours
Worked Example (NORCET 9 PYQ): 60 kg patient, 20% TBSA → 4 × 60 × 20 = 4800 mL total → 2400 mL in first 8h → 2400 mL in next 16h.

Key Burn Management Points

  • Fluid of choice: Lactated Ringer's (RL)
  • PRIORITY: Airway first — suspect inhalation injury with facial/nasal burns, singed nasal hair, carbonaceous sputum
  • Curling's ulcer: Stress ulcer after burns → prophylactic PPIs (esomeprazole)
  • Circumferential burns: Risk of compartment syndrome → escharotomy
  • Wound care: Silver sulfadiazine (topical)
  • Tetanus prophylaxis routinely
  • Urine output target: 0.5 mL/kg/hr (adults) — best fluid resuscitation indicator
