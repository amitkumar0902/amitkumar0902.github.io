2 hours

Newborn Resuscitation (NRP)

Initial Rapid Evaluation (Golden Minute)

  • Term? Tone? Breathing/crying?
  • If all YES → routine care with mother (skin-to-skin, dry, warm)
  • If any NO → begin initial steps

NRP Algorithm

  1. Airway: Warm, dry, stimulate, position (sniffing position), suction mouth-then-nose if obstructed
  2. Breathing: If apneic / gasping / HR <100 → PPV within 60 seconds (40–60 breaths/min) — this is the most important step
  3. Circulation: If HR <60 despite 30 sec of effective PPV → chest compressions (compression-to-ventilation 3:1, total 120 events/min)
  4. Drugs: Epinephrine IV/IO if HR <60 despite 60 sec of CPR + PPV. Volume expander (NS or O-neg blood) if hypovolemic.
Targeted SpO₂ (pre-ductal, right hand):
1 min — 60–65%   ·   2 min — 65–70%   ·   3 min — 70–75%   ·   4 min — 75–80%   ·   5 min — 80–85%   ·   10 min — 85–95%
Ventilation is the single most important step in neonatal resuscitation — the vast majority of neonates respond to effective PPV.

APGAR Score

APGAR = Appearance · Pulse · Grimace · Activity · Respiration
Component012
Appearance (color)Blue/pale all overBody pink, extremities blue (acrocyanosis)Completely pink
Pulse MOST IMPORTANTAbsent<100>100
Grimace (reflex)No responseGrimaceCough / sneeze / cry
Activity (tone)LimpSome flexionActive motion
RespirationAbsentSlow / irregular / weak cryStrong cry

Timing & Interpretation

  • Assessed at 1 minute and 5 minutes of life
  • Continue at 10 min if 5-min score ≤ 6
  • 7–10 → Normal, routine care
  • 4–6 → Moderate distress, some resuscitation
  • 0–3 → Severe distress, full resuscitation
  • Heart rate is the single most important parameter
1.5 hours

RDS — Respiratory Distress Syndrome

Cause & Risk Factors

  • Surfactant deficiency — most common in preterm (<34 weeks)
  • Risk: Prematurity, maternal diabetes, C-section without labour, male sex, 2nd twin

Clinical Signs (Silverman & Downe scoring — NORCET 9 PYQ)

  • Tachypnea (RR >60)
  • Nasal flaring
  • Expiratory grunting
  • Intercostal / subcostal retractions
  • Cyanosis (late)
  • Chest X-ray: ground-glass appearance with air bronchograms

Management

  • Surfactant replacement (Survanta / Curosurf) via ETT
  • CPAP or mechanical ventilation as indicated
  • Thermal regulation, IV fluids, gentle handling
  • Prevention: Antenatal Betamethasone to mother before preterm delivery

Complications

  • Pneumothorax
  • BPD (Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia)
  • ROP (Retinopathy of Prematurity) — from high-flow O₂
  • IVH (Intraventricular Hemorrhage)
NORCET PYQ link: High-flow oxygen in premature → risk of Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP).
