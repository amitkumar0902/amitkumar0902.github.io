Priority-Based Assessment

Golden Rules (Order of Priority)

  1. ABCs: Airway > Breathing > Circulation (always first)
  2. Maslow's Hierarchy: Physiologic > Safety > Love/belonging > Esteem > Self-actualization
  3. Acute over chronic: New abnormal finding > expected chronic finding
  4. Unstable over stable: Chest pain > routine post-op > stable chronic
  5. Actual over potential: Existing problem > risk for problem

Delegation — RN vs UAP vs LPN

RN CANNOT delegate:
  • Assessment
  • Teaching / patient education
  • Evaluation of care
  • Care planning / nursing diagnosis
  • Unstable patients
UAP CAN do: Vital signs on stable patients, bathing, ambulation, I&O, positioning, feeding non-dysphagic patients, specimen collection.
LPN CAN do (supervised): Stable patient care, sterile dressing changes, medication administration (most states), reinforcement of teaching.

Advanced Drug Calculations

Heparin Drip

Bolus: 80 units/kg   |   Infusion: 18 units/kg/hr
Rate (mL/hr): (units/hr × 500 mL) / 25,000 units
  • Monitoring: aPTT every 6h — therapeutic 1.5–2.5× control
  • NORCET PYQ: aPTT 85 sec → continue heparin at current rate (within therapeutic range 60–90s)
  • Antidote: Protamine sulfate 1 mg reverses 100 units of heparin
  • Critical labs: Platelets (watch for HIT)

Vasopressor / Dopamine Drip — mcg/kg/min

mL/hr = (mcg/kg/min × kg × 60) / concentration (mcg/mL)
  • Dopamine doses: Low (1–5 mcg/kg/min) renal · Moderate (5–10) inotropic · High (>10) vasopressor
  • Noradrenaline: 0.05–2 mcg/kg/min — preferred in septic shock

Insulin Drip

  • Typical concentration: 100 units / 100 mL NS → 1 unit/mL
  • DKA: 0.1 unit/kg/hr infusion; hold if K⁺ < 3.3
  • Check glucose every hour

Pediatric / High-Alert

  • Always verify mg/kg/dose is within safe range
  • Double-check high-alert meds: Insulin, heparin, chemotherapy, opioids, potassium, magnesium (NORCET 7 PYQ)
  • Potassium IV: Never bolus · dilute · peripheral max 10 mEq/hr, central max 20 mEq/hr · use infusion pump

Cardiac Emergencies — ACLS / BLS

BLS Sequence (Adult)

C-A-B: Compressions → Airway → Breathing · Ratio 30:2 · Depth 2–2.4 inches · Rate 100–120/min · Minimize interruptions <10 sec

Code Cart Arrhythmia Algorithms

RhythmManagement
VT with pulse (stable)Amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min; correct electrolytes
VT with pulse (unstable)Synchronized cardioversion (start 100 J)
Pulseless VT / VFDefibrillation (unsynchronized 200 J biphasic) + CPR + Epi 1 mg IV after 3rd shock + Amiodarone 300 mg
Asystole / PEACPR + Epi 1 mg IV every 3–5 min · Identify H's and T's · NO defibrillation
Symptomatic bradycardiaAtropine 1 mg IV (first-line) → transcutaneous pacing → Epi/dopamine drip
SVT (stable)Vagal maneuvers → Adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push (then 12 mg)
SVT (unstable)Synchronized cardioversion

H's and T's (Reversible Causes)

H's: Hypovolemia · Hypoxia · H⁺ (acidosis) · Hyper/Hypokalemia · Hypothermia · Hypoglycemia
T's: Tension pneumothorax · Tamponade · Toxins · Thrombosis (coronary/pulmonary) · Trauma

High-Yield Drug Points

  • Epinephrine in arrest: 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 min (NORCET 9 PYQ: "after 3rd shock")
  • Amiodarone: 300 mg bolus in pulseless VT/VF, then 150 mg
  • Digoxin: Hold if apical pulse <60; monitor K⁺ (hypoK worsens tox); antidote Digibind
  • Atropine: symptomatic bradycardia · NOT for asystole in ACLS (2010 update removed)
  • Calcium gluconate: Hyperkalemia with peaked T waves — stabilizes membrane
