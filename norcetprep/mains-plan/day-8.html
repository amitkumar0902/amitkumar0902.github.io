TORCH Infections — CMV & Toxoplasmosis

TORCH: Toxoplasmosis · Other (syphilis, VZV, parvovirus) · Rubella · CMV · Herpes/HIV

CMV (Cytomegalovirus)

  • Most common congenital viral infection worldwide
  • Transmission: body fluids (saliva, urine, blood, breast milk, sexual), transplacental
  • Congenital effects: Sensorineural hearing loss (most common sequela), microcephaly, periventricular calcifications, jaundice, hepatosplenomegaly, "blueberry muffin" rash
  • Diagnosis: PCR; CMV-IgM / IgG
  • Treatment: Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir in severe disease

Toxoplasmosis

  • Caused by Toxoplasma gondii
  • Sources: undercooked meat, cat litter / cat feces, unwashed produce
  • Pregnant women should avoid cleaning cat litter & wash fruits/vegetables thoroughly
  • Classic triad (congenital): Chorioretinitis · Hydrocephalus · Intracranial calcifications (diffuse)
  • Diagnosis: IgM/IgG serology, amniocentesis PCR
  • Treatment: Spiramycin in early pregnancy; Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid for confirmed fetal infection

Enema Procedure

Positioning & Technique

  • Position: Left lateral (Sims) with right leg flexed
  • Insertion depth (adult): 7.5 – 10 cm (3–4 inches)
  • Pediatric: 2.5 – 7.5 cm depending on age
  • Solution temperature: Warm 40–43°C (105–110°F)
  • Container height: 12 inches (30 cm) above anus for cleansing enema
  • If cramping: Stop flow · lower container · instruct deep breathing

1.5 hours

Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

  • Route: Central line ONLY (hyperosmolar >900 mOsm/L)
  • Change tubing: Every 24 hours (risk of sepsis)
  • Bag hang time: Maximum 24 hours
  • If TPN interrupted: Hang D10W at same rate to prevent rebound hypoglycemia
  • Never add medications to TPN bag
  • Never increase/decrease rate abruptly

Monitoring Priorities

  • Blood glucose — every 6h (hyperglycemia is most common complication)
  • Daily weight, strict I&O
  • Electrolytes, BUN/creatinine, LFTs
  • Serum protein / prealbumin every 3–7 days
  • Watch for refeeding syndrome in malnourished patients (low K, Mg, Phos)

Complications

  • Infection / sepsis (most serious)
  • Hyperglycemia / rebound hypoglycemia
  • Air embolism — clamp line · turn patient to LEFT side + Trendelenburg
  • Fluid overload / electrolyte imbalance
  • Fat embolism (with lipid infusion)
  • Hepatic steatosis (long-term)
1 hour

Central Line Care

Common Insertion Sites

  • Subclavian — most common (lowest infection rate)
  • Internal jugular — easier ultrasound-guided
  • Femoral — highest infection risk; emergency use
  • Tip position: Superior Vena Cava (SVC) — confirm with chest X-ray before first use

Post-Insertion Complications

  • Pneumothorax — always check chest X-ray
  • Air embolism — turn LEFT side + Trendelenburg, clamp catheter, administer 100% O₂
  • Hemothorax, arterial puncture
  • CLABSI (Central Line-Associated Blood Stream Infection)

CLABSI Prevention Bundle (High-Yield)

  1. Hand hygiene
  2. Maximal barrier precautions at insertion (cap, mask, gown, gloves, full drape)
  3. Chlorhexidine skin antisepsis
  4. Optimal site selection (subclavian > IJ > femoral)
  5. Daily review of line necessity — remove ASAP
  6. Scrub the hub for 15 seconds before each access
  7. Sterile dressing change every 7 days (transparent) or 2 days (gauze), or when soiled
NORCET PYQ (Triple-lumen catheter): Know the distal/proximal/middle ports and their purposes. Never draw blood from TPN port.
