The 1:4 Rule — PYQ Study Method

The difference between a rank holder and a pass holder is how they solve PYQs — not how many.

For EVERY PYQ solved:

  1. Verify the correct answer from a standard textbook or guideline (Lewis, Brunner & Suddarth, Dutta, Ghai, Park)
  2. Research all 3 wrong options — AIIMS rotates distractors into future correct answers. A wrong option today is likely a right option tomorrow.
  3. Write the concept in a one-page error log: the correct answer + the 3 distractor concepts + why each is wrong
  4. Link back to the relevant day-page in this plan (e.g., ABG → Day 1)
  5. Flag questions you guessed correctly — these are weak areas in disguise and need revision

Why "1:4"?

For every 1 answer you verify, you study 4 concepts (1 correct + 3 distractors). This turns each PYQ into 4 learning opportunities — dramatically expanding your conceptual bandwidth.

Sample Error Log Entry

Q: Eclampsia drug of choice?
Options: (a) Diazepam (b) MgSO₄ (c) Phenytoin (d) Labetalol
Correct: (b) MgSO₄ — anticonvulsant, therapeutic 4–7 mEq/L, antidote Ca gluconate
Distractor research:
(a) Diazepam — outdated for eclampsia; still used in status epilepticus (non-eclamptic)
(c) Phenytoin — 2nd-line if MgSO₄ fails or contraindicated
(d) Labetalol — 1st-line for BP in preeclampsia, NOT for seizure prevention
